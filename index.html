Physics & computational research

Edward Troccoli

I study quantum systems and the numerical methods that help us understand them.

I'm a physics graduate from the Australian National University, where I completed a Bachelor of Philosophy (Honours) in Science in October 2026. My Honours research explored parallel fast entangling gates for trapped-ion quantum computers, supervised by Professor Joseph Hope and Dr Zain Mehdi.

My interests centre on quantum computing, numerical optimisation and computational physics. I'm particularly interested in how physical constraints and control methods shape the performance and scalability of quantum computers.

I've also worked in computational astrophysics, studying how energy dissipates in turbulent flows with Professor Christoph Federrath. That work led to my first-author publication in MNRAS.

Quantum computing

Parallel fast entangling gates

Can fast entangling gates be implemented in parallel to improve the scalability of trapped-ion quantum computers? My Honours project explored this question through numerical optimisation.

Project details +

I independently implemented an optimisation algorithm for parallel fast gates, building on ideas developed in the research group. My theoretical and numerical studies considered two simultaneous two-qubit gates in a 10-ion chain, including the suppression of crosstalk. I compared gate time, fidelity and pulse resources with serial execution: the benefits of parallel operation depend on the ion-pair geometry and control constraints.

Supervisors Professor Joseph Hope and Dr Zain Mehdi · ANU
Thesis Parallel Fast Entangling Gates for Trapped Ion Quantum Computing · Submitted October 2026

Quantum control

Making fast gates more robust

I used numerical optimisation and phase control to design state-dependent kicks with up to two orders of magnitude lower infidelity under laser timing jitter than the benchmark scheme.

Project details +

This Advanced Studies Course project investigated how phase control can cancel errors in state-dependent kicks used for fast trapped-ion gates. The results come from numerical simulations; the supplied document is a 2025 research manuscript.

Supervisors Professor Joseph Hope and Dr Zain Mehdi · ANU

Computational astrophysics

Where turbulent energy dissipates

Studying the structure and statistics of kinetic-energy dissipation in subsonic and supersonic turbulence, using high-resolution simulations and numerical analysis.

Project details +

I ran hydrodynamic simulations with FLASH on high-performance computing systems, independently developed Python routines for Fourier spectral and fractal-dimension analysis, and led the manuscript writing. Professor Christoph Federrath implemented the dissipation method in FLASH.

Supervisor Professor Christoph Federrath · ANU
Outcome First-author publication in Monthly Notices of the Royal Astronomical Society (2026).

Fluid dynamics

Shear instabilities in confined fluids

I developed an analytical model of laterally confined Kelvin–Helmholtz instabilities and compared its predictions with laboratory observations.

Project details +

The project examined how viscosity, finite fluid depth and surface tension affect shear instabilities. I used Python to fit an effective surface-tension parameter to the experimental data, and explored the limits of the model at the scale of the experiment.

Supervisor Dr Callum Shakespeare · ANU
Report On Laterally Confined Kelvin-Helmholtz Instabilities · Summer Session 2024

Optics & experimental physics

Lasers & fibre frequency references

Experimental projects on the thermal response of fibre frequency-reference packaging and the optimisation of an external-cavity laser for ultracold-helium experiments.

Project details +

For the fibre frequency-reference winter project, supervised by Ya Zhang, I compared resin packaging under controlled temperature cycling. The presentation reports amplitude and phase measurements relevant to precision references for the GRACE missions.

In a separate 2024 project with Dr Sean Hodgman, I optimised an existing external-cavity laser and its control electronics. This included diagnosing electronic noise, calibrating monitoring electronics and measuring the laser's power–current response.

Edward Troccoli and Christoph Federrath

Paper PDF ↗DOI ↗
About this paper +

We use high-resolution hydrodynamic simulations to investigate how kinetic energy dissipates in subsonic and supersonic turbulent flows. The study examines the time lag between energy injection and dissipation, correlations with flow structure, dissipation spectra and the fractal geometry of dissipative regions.
